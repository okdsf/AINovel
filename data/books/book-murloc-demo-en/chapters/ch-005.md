It was 1:02 AM when I went down to the basement.

I was wearing my oldest sneakers — the ones with the softest soles — so the stairs wouldn't creak.

I didn't turn on the light. I used my phone screen.

The murloc was next to my bicycle. Same spot as last night. Its eyes were closed. But when the light from my phone hit it, it opened one eye — just one — and then closed it again.

As if to say: oh, it's you.

I crouched down. We were very close. I could smell something on it now, a faint dampness — somewhere between a swimming-pool locker room and a school field after rain.

I cleared my throat.

"Hey," I said.

It didn't respond.

"I need to talk to you."

It opened the other eye.

I took a deep breath.

"…I can't let anyone know you're in my house," I said. "Do you understand?"

It "mrrgglglglgl"ed.

I didn't know if that was *yes* or *no*. I wasn't sure it distinguished.

"If anyone asks," I said, "if **anyone** asks — police, journalists, my mom, my friends, the TA who's been trying to butter up our physics advisor — **you cannot speak**."

"mrrgglglglgl."

"You cannot speak, because you **don't speak**. You only make that one sound. So this part should not be hard."

"mrrgglglglgl."

"Good."

We looked at each other for a few more seconds.

I became aware that this was a very strange conversation. I became aware that the imbalance of it was that I believed it understood me — even though it almost certainly did not. Maybe it just found the light from my phone interesting. Maybe it really was just a murloc from a video game, accidentally let out of a door by someone, with no comprehension whatsoever of human language or concepts.

Maybe it was waiting for me to finish this monologue, "mrrgglglglgl" one more time, and continue sleeping against my bicycle.

But.

If it really understood.

If on some level it understood.

If on some level it understood that **I** was the one who let it out —

Then the silent pact between us might be the most important asset I had from this point forward.

I pulled a piece of braised pork from my pocket — one I had pocketed during dinner, wrapped in a napkin. I placed it on the floor next to my bicycle.

It looked at the pork. Two eyes open.

It didn't move.

I waited.

It still didn't move.

I stood up and took two steps backwards toward the stairs.

It reached out with one small blue hand — four fingers, with a little webbing between each — and picked up the pork.

It bit down.

Then it "mrrgglglglgl"ed again, but in a different tone this time. A tone that reminded me of something I'd heard in some kids' cartoon once — the soft, almost whimper-like sound a child makes from deep in their throat upon receiving a Christmas present they'd actually wanted.

I went back up the stairs.

I closed the basement door gently.

I went back to my own bedroom, took off my shoes, and climbed into bed.

I stared at the ceiling.

The murloc was quiet downstairs.

The TV — I hadn't turned it off before going down — was still on. Volume low. Barely the outline of sound floating up through the floor. Anderson Cooper still asking. Fox News still shouting. I could imagine, two hours from now, Sarah Lin still standing in the White House press office telling everyone "we are coordinating with relevant agencies."

The whole country, since this morning, has been waiting for an answer.

The answer is in my basement.

I closed my eyes.

I decided I would sleep first.
