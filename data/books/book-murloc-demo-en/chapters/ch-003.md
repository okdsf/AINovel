I spent the afternoon in my room.

I did two things.

**First**: I scrolled the hashtag `#rosegardenmurloc` on Twitter. It started trending around 9 AM. By 4 PM it had overtaken what should have been the day's biggest story — some governor breaking a pen during a signing ceremony — and become the top trend in the United States.

The most-liked tweet was this:

> While the entire world is asking "Where did it come from?", I just want to say one thing: **it is really, really cute**.
>
> (Image attached: a screenshot of the murloc sitting in the rose bed, looking genuinely confused)
>
> 124K likes · 37K retweets

I stared at my empty retweet button. I didn't click. There is **absolutely no way** my account is going anywhere near this thing. Not even a like.

**Second thing**: I downloaded an app for deleting Twitter accounts, hesitated for eleven minutes, then didn't use it.

At 5:30 PM, Mom called me up for dinner.

I went downstairs. Our dining table is, as a rule, TV-free. Mom's rule. But she'd broken it tonight. The TV was on, volume low, but the chyron was big.

The chyron said: "WHITE HOUSE PRESS SECRETARY: 'WE ARE COORDINATING WITH RELEVANT AGENCIES.'"

"What's that?" Mom said, setting down the braised pork, glancing at the TV. "That little blue thing?"

"Yeah," I said.

"It's kind of cute."

I picked up a piece with my chopsticks and didn't say anything.

"Do you know what it is?" Mom asked.

I looked up at her.

She was opening a can of beer. Her attention was distributed across three things: the TV, the can, getting the bowls into the right configuration. She wasn't really asking me. She was talking to herself.

"…no," I said. "Looks kind of like a murloc. You know. From games."

"From games?" Mom said, sitting down. "How does something from a game get into the Rose Garden?"

"I don't know either."

I started eating the braised pork. I ate slowly. I ate with concentration. I ate with something approaching ritual focus.

Mom took a sip of beer. "What a strange world we live in."

I picked up another piece of pork.

The CNN reporter was still outside the West Wing — he'd been standing there all day, visibly exhausted but visibly committed. He said: "What we can confirm is — the creature is no longer in the Rose Garden."

My chopsticks paused.

It was **no longer** in the Rose Garden.

So where was it?

I glanced at the basement door.

No sound from below.

The door looked the same as it did this morning. Plain wood. Calm.

"How's the pork?" Mom asked.

"Really good," I said.

My throat was a little dry.
