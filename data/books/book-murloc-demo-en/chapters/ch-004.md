After dinner I helped Mom with the dishes.

This is something I had not done in years. Mom glanced at me with a "did you break something" look but said nothing. I washed each dish very carefully. I washed for what must have been fourteen minutes. The last two bowls did not actually need washing for that long.

At 8 PM Mom turned on the living-room TV. *Anderson Cooper 360°*.

Anderson Cooper looked even more tired than the field reporter outside the West Wing this morning, but in his eyes there was that thing I'd only ever seen before in documentaries — that **never-give-up** look. The kind of look that said: we are not stopping tonight until we have an answer.

He faced the camera and said:

> Ladies and gentlemen, tonight we still do not know: Where did this creature come from? Where is it now? Does it understand English? Does the President know?

I stared at the TV.

I stared at Anderson Cooper.

I stared at a barely-visible crease on the second button of his shirt.

> But tonight, CNN has several **exclusive** sources—

My palms started sweating.

> —who tell us: at approximately 3:14 AM, just before the Rose Garden incident, a research lab at a university in the western United States triggered an unusual electromagnetic event.

That's not the west. That's **here**.

It's the lab in my own basement.

Well. It's the lab whose physics setup ended up in my basement. The two things are technically the same lab right now, but — that's a long story.

> The university has given **three different** official statements in the last twelve hours.

I counted. Three. The university is the one I attend. It's their physics department's auxiliary lab. The reason I know this is —

Doesn't matter. Cannot matter.

Anderson Cooper is interviewing a "former FBI agent" — name withheld, face blurred — who is saying, in the most theatrical voice he can muster: "We have a protocol for bears. We have a protocol for alligators. We have a protocol for swarms. We do not have a protocol for this."

Mom yawned. "News is becoming more and more like TV drama these days."

"Mm."

"Want to go to bed early? You look tired."

"I'll watch a little more."

"Mom's going up."

Mom turned off her side lamp and went upstairs.

I sat alone on the couch.

The TV was on Fox News now — not because I changed the channel, but because of a CNN commercial break. A bow-tied host on Fox was shouting: "We do not have an answer tonight — is this the level of accountability the American people deserve?"

Behind him a graphic was playing on loop: the murloc's silhouette circled in red, set to a low frenetic string score.

I watched for a while.

Then it struck me —

If **any** investigation seriously tracked the lab back to that one specific room —

> The borrow-sheet for that lab on Wednesday last week has **only my name on it**.

I turned the TV off.

The living room went very quiet.

I stared at the basement door.

No sound from below.

But I **knew** the murloc was down there.

I knew.
