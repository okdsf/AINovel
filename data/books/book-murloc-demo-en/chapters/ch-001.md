3:11 AM.

The basement.

I just burned that godforsaken book. I used the baking tray Mom uses for her Christmas turkey, lined with foil — the safest configuration I could think of for not setting off the smoke alarm. It burns slow. But it burns.

I stared at the small orange flame for a while. The pages went black one curl at a time.

About halfway through the burn, the door — not the wooden basement door, the *other* door — opened.

I'll admit I didn't see it clearly.

But something landed with a wet, mop-on-tile sort of sound. Then it stood up. Knee-high on me. The color was that very specific kind of blue you used to see in early-nineties CRT monitor menus.

It saw me.

I saw it.

We watched each other for what I'd estimate as three full seconds. Then it said something. Or made a noise. Approximately:

"mrrgglglglgl."

I can't tell you exactly what my first thought was. Or rather — my first thought was too long to be a thought. But broadly:

> It's over.

> It's really over.

> Every single thing you have ever vaguely worried about going wrong has, indeed, gone wrong — and the thing is now in your basement, looking at you, waiting for you to explain.

The door — not the wooden basement door, the *other* one — closed maybe three seconds after it came through. Very softly. A small *click*. The kind of click you'd associate with someone gently closing a laptop they thought might be asleep.

And then it was just me and — whatever this was.

It "mrrgglglglgl"ed again. Slightly quieter this time. Almost tentative.

I slowly moved the baking tray away from the burning book. Because I suddenly realized that if it felt threatened by me, I would have no way to explain why I was holding fire.

It looked me over. Then it did the thing that I would later return to, over and over, lying in bed for nights afterwards:

It waddled over to my old bicycle, **sat down**, leaned its head against the frame, and closed its eyes.

Like it was finally home.

I crouched down at one-third speed. We sat like that, the two of us, at 3:14 AM in the basement, with the last of the smoke still curling up from my baking tray.

Upstairs, Mom turned over in her sleep. She doesn't know.

She **cannot** know.
